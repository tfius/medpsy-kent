# Serotonin syndrome — interaction screening

> DEMO CORPUS — illustrative content for retrieval evaluation; not a clinical reference.

## High-risk combinations
- **SSRI/SNRI + triptan** (e.g. sertraline + sumatriptan): generally low absolute risk but
  counsel on early symptoms; avoid initiating both simultaneously.
- **SSRI + tramadol**: tramadol is a serotonin reuptake inhibitor and lowers seizure
  threshold — a common, genuinely risky OTC-adjacent request after dental work.
- **MAOI + any serotonergic agent** (SSRIs, pethidine, dextromethorphan): contraindicated;
  requires a 14-day washout after stopping an irreversible MAOI.
- **St John's Wort** with any antidepressant — herbal status hides a real pharmacological
  interaction; also induces CYP3A4 (oral contraceptive failure).
- **Linezolid** (an antibiotic with MAOI activity) + SSRI — often missed at discharge.

## Recognition
Triad of (1) neuromuscular excitation: clonus, hyperreflexia, tremor; (2) autonomic
instability: fever, sweating, tachycardia; (3) altered mental state: agitation, confusion.
Onset is typically within 24 h of dose change — distinguishes it from neuroleptic
malignant syndrome (days).

## Action
Mild symptoms after a new combination: stop the offending agent, same-day GP review.
Fever >38.5 °C, rigidity, or confusion: emergency — refer to ED immediately.
