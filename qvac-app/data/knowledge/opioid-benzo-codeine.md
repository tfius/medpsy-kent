# Opioids with CNS depressants — respiratory depression risk

> DEMO CORPUS — illustrative content for retrieval evaluation; not a clinical reference.

## The combination that kills
Opioid + benzodiazepine (or z-drug, gabapentinoid, alcohol) is the most common pattern in
fatal accidental overdose: synergistic respiratory depression. Any patient picking up both
deserves a check — especially new co-prescriptions, dose increases, or "borrowed" tablets.

## Counter scenarios
- Codeine-containing OTC analgesics requested by a patient on diazepam, zopiclone,
  pregabalin, or with alcohol on board: avoid; offer non-opioid alternatives.
- Post-operative oxycodone + pre-existing benzodiazepine: confirm the prescriber knew;
  counsel household on overdose signs.
- Elderly with renal impairment: opioid accumulation (morphine, codeine) — drowsiness
  building over days is a warning, not "settling in."

## Overdose recognition
Unrousable or pinpoint pupils with slow/shallow breathing (<8/min), cyanosis, snoring/
gurgling respiration: emergency — call EMS, give **naloxone** if available (it wears off
faster than most opioids; repeat dosing may be needed).

## Codeine notes
Ultra-rapid CYP2D6 metabolisers convert codeine to morphine unpredictably — codeine is
contraindicated under 12 years and in breastfeeding mothers.
