# Metformin — lactic acidosis risk and sick-day rules

> DEMO CORPUS — illustrative content for retrieval evaluation; not a clinical reference.

## Lactic acidosis
Rare but life-threatening. Risk concentrates where renal clearance falls or lactate
production rises:
- **eGFR**: review dose below 45 mL/min/1.73 m²; **stop below 30**.
- **Iodinated contrast media**: withhold metformin on the day of the scan and for 48 h
  after if renal function is reduced; restart once renal function is confirmed stable.
- **Acute illness with dehydration** (vomiting, diarrhoea, sepsis): temporary cessation —
  the classic "sick-day rule" drug (with ACE inhibitors, diuretics, NSAIDs: "SADMAN").
- **Heavy alcohol**: both raises lactate and risks hypoglycaemia; binge drinking on
  metformin warrants specific counselling.

## Recognition of lactic acidosis
Nonspecific and easily missed: deep rapid breathing, abdominal pain, muscle cramps,
unusual drowsiness in a metformin patient who is acutely unwell — emergency referral.

## Common, benign effects (do not escalate)
GI upset on initiation (take with food, titrate slowly), B12 deficiency on long-term use
(check levels if neuropathy or anaemia), metallic taste. Metformin alone does **not**
cause hypoglycaemia.
