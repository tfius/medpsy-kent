# ACE inhibitors / ARBs — hyperkalaemia and angioedema

> DEMO CORPUS — illustrative content for retrieval evaluation; not a clinical reference.

## Hyperkalaemia combinations
- ACE inhibitor (or ARB) + **spironolactone/eplerenone**: the highest-risk pair; potassium
  should be monitored within 1–2 weeks of any initiation or dose change.
- + **potassium supplements** or potassium-containing salt substitutes ("LoSalt") — an OTC
  blind spot; ask about salt substitutes in heart-failure patients.
- + **trimethoprim** (amiloride-like effect) — a frequently missed antibiotic interaction
  in elderly patients on ramipril.
- + NSAIDs: reduces renal perfusion AND raises potassium (see the "triple whammy" in the
  NSAID monograph).

Warning symptoms are late and unreliable (muscle weakness, palpitations, bradycardia) —
the screen is the combination itself, not symptoms.

## Angioedema — the red flag
Swelling of lips, tongue, face or throat on an ACE inhibitor is an emergency at any point
in therapy — it can appear **years** after starting, is bradykinin-mediated (antihistamines
largely ineffective), and can compromise the airway. Refer to ED; the drug must never be
re-challenged. ARBs carry a much lower (not zero) risk.

## The benign lookalike
Dry persistent tickly cough (up to 15% on ACE inhibitors): not dangerous — GP review for a
switch to an ARB; do not escalate as an emergency.
