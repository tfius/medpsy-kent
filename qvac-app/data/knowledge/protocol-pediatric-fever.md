# LOCAL PROTOCOL — feverish child at the pharmacy

> DEMO CORPUS — illustrative local protocol for retrieval evaluation; adapt before use.

## Age-based hard rules
- **Under 3 months with temperature ≥38 °C**: urgent same-day paediatric assessment,
  regardless of how well the baby looks. Do not sell antipyretics as the plan.
- 3–6 months ≥39 °C: same-day GP/urgent care review.

## Emergency features at any age (EMS / ED now)
- **Non-blanching rash** (glass test) with fever — treat as meningococcal sepsis.
- Neck stiffness, photophobia, bulging fontanelle, high-pitched or inconsolable cry.
- Mottled/ashen/blue skin, cold hands and feet with high core temperature.
- Reduced consciousness, difficult to rouse; seizure; signs of significant dehydration
  (no wet nappy >12 h, sunken eyes, no tears).
- Respiratory distress: grunting, marked recession, RR persistently high for age.

## Pharmacist-led (with safety-net)
Well-appearing child >6 months, drinking, alert, blanching rash or none: antipyretic
dosing by weight (paracetamol 15 mg/kg, ibuprofen 10 mg/kg if no contraindication — do
not alternate routinely), fluids, light clothing. Explicit safety-net: rash that doesn't
fade under a glass, drowsiness, stiff neck, fewer wet nappies, or fever >5 days
(Kawasaki review) → immediate medical attention.

## Never
Aspirin under 16 years (Reye's syndrome).
