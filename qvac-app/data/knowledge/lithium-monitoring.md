# Lithium — narrow therapeutic index in the pharmacy

> DEMO CORPUS — illustrative content for retrieval evaluation; not a clinical reference.

## What pushes lithium up
Lithium is renally cleared and behaves like sodium — anything that makes the kidney retain
sodium retains lithium:
- **NSAIDs** (including OTC ibuprofen — the classic pharmacy near-miss),
- **Thiazide diuretics** (loop diuretics less so),
- **ACE inhibitors/ARBs**,
- **Dehydration**: vomiting/diarrhoea, fever, heatwaves, low-salt crash diets.
A lithium patient buying ibuprofen for a feverish flu in summer stacks three risk factors.

## Toxicity recognition
Early: coarse tremor (vs the fine tremor of therapeutic lithium), nausea, diarrhoea.
Progressing: ataxia, slurred speech, confusion, muscle twitching. Severe: seizures, renal
failure, arrhythmia. Levels >1.5 mmol/L are toxic; >2.5 mmol/L can need dialysis.
Toxicity can develop on a **stable dose** when intercurrent illness changes clearance.

## Counter guidance
Analgesia for lithium patients: paracetamol. Maintain fluid and salt intake during illness;
any vomiting/diarrhoea >24 h warrants a level check and possible temporary cessation —
same-day GP/111 contact, ED if neurological signs are present.
