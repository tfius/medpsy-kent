# LOCAL PROTOCOL — chest pain presenting at the pharmacy

> DEMO CORPUS — illustrative local protocol for retrieval evaluation; adapt before use.

## Immediate EMS (call emergency services from the pharmacy — do not send home or to GP)
- Chest pain/pressure/tightness lasting **>15 minutes**, or ongoing at presentation.
- Pain radiating to jaw, neck, shoulder or arm; associated sweating, nausea, or
  breathlessness.
- **Atypical presentations count**: diabetics, women, and the elderly may have NO chest
  pain — jaw/back ache, profound fatigue, nausea and sweating alone justify EMS.
- Chest pain with syncope, or new tearing pain to the back (possible dissection).

While waiting: patient sits, 300 mg aspirin chewed if no allergy/active bleeding and
myocardial infarction suspected; do not leave the patient unattended; have the
defibrillator location known.

## Same-day urgent (GP/urgent care, not EMS)
- Brief resolved chest pain in a low-risk patient with normal observations and no red
  flags, especially if exertional — needs same-day assessment, not reassurance.

## Pharmacist-led (with safety-net)
- Reproducible chest-wall tenderness after exertion/strain, young patient, no risk
  factors, pain altered by movement/breathing position: likely musculoskeletal. Analgesia
  and explicit safety-net: any pressure-like pain, breathlessness, or pain at rest → EMS.
- Burning retrosternal pain after meals, relieved by antacids: reflux pathway; alarm
  features (dysphagia, weight loss, GI bleeding, age >55 new-onset) → referral instead.
