# LOCAL PROTOCOL — head injury in anticoagulated / antiplatelet patients

> DEMO CORPUS — illustrative local protocol for retrieval evaluation; adapt before use.

## The rule
**Any head injury — however trivial — in a patient on anticoagulation (warfarin, DOACs:
apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban, dabigatran) or dual antiplatelets goes to the Emergency
Department for assessment**, even with no symptoms and no visible injury. Intracranial
bleeding can be delayed by hours to days; "I feel fine" does not clear them.

This includes: bumps on door frames/cupboards, falls from standing without loss of
consciousness, minor car-park trips. The threshold is the injury *plus the drug*, not the
severity of impact.

## High-urgency features (EMS, not own transport)
Loss of consciousness, repeated vomiting, worsening headache, confusion or drowsiness,
seizure, clear fluid or blood from the ear/nose, unequal pupils, weakness or numbness.

## Everyone else (not anticoagulated)
Minor head injury, fully alert, none of the above features: home with a written
head-injury advice sheet and a responsible adult for 24 h; symptoms emerging → ED.
Elderly patients and those with prior brain surgery or alcohol dependence: lower the
referral threshold.

## Pharmacy notes
Falls in warfarinised elderly patients are also a prompt to flag falls-risk review and
INR check — log the encounter and notify the GP with the patient's consent.
