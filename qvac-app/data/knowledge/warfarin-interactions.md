# Warfarin — key pharmacy interactions

> DEMO CORPUS — illustrative content for retrieval evaluation; not a clinical reference.
> Verify against the local formulary / BNF before clinical use.

## NSAIDs and aspirin
Concurrent NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac) or aspirin markedly increase bleeding
risk: antiplatelet effect plus gastric mucosal injury on top of anticoagulation. Avoid OTC
NSAID sale to warfarin patients; recommend paracetamol for analgesia (note: regular
paracetamol >2 g/day for >3 days can itself raise INR — advise INR check).

## Antibiotics
- **Macrolides (clarithromycin, erythromycin)** — inhibit CYP3A4; INR rises within days.
- **Metronidazole** and **trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole** — potent potentiators; many
  protocols pre-emptively reduce warfarin dose 25–50% and recheck INR in 3–5 days.
- **Rifampicin** — strong inducer; INR falls, thrombosis risk.
Any new antibiotic on warfarin warrants an INR check within 3–7 days.

## Other common potentiators
Amiodarone, fluconazole/miconazole (including **oral gel** — frequently missed OTC),
cranberry juice in quantity, acute alcohol binge.

## Red flags — escalate
Black/tarry stools, coffee-ground vomit, unexplained extensive bruising, blood in urine,
severe headache (possible intracranial bleed): urgent medical assessment, same day.
